Context & my role

Growing companies, disconnected planning

Each client had the same pattern. Leadership planned in one tool, engineers worked in another, and someone spent hours every week reconciling the two into status reports. With 15–20 initiatives running at once, prioritization came down to whoever had the latest spreadsheet.

Problem & constraints

Reporting overhead and no shared view

Most status reporting followed the same pattern every week: pull the same fields, summarize the same things. That made it worth automating, as long as accuracy and accountability stayed with a person.

Planning in Monday.com, execution in Jira, with no sync between them
12 separate reporting sources reconciled by hand
PM time going to assembling status instead of resolving issues
No formal decision rights for prioritizing initiatives
Key decisions

Two calls that shaped the work

One system of record per field, not two-way sync on everything

Options
(a) Full two-way sync between Monday.com and Jira · (b) Assign each field one owning system and flag conflicts
Chose
One owning system per field, with conflicts flagged
Tradeoff
Some updates now had to be made in a specific tool. In return, the sync never silently overwrote someone's change, which was the main reason teams trusted it and kept using it (90% adoption).

AI drafts the report, a person approves it

Options
(a) Keep writing reports by hand · (b) Publish LLM summaries automatically · (c) LLM writes a draft from structured fields, and the PM reviews and publishes
Chose
LLM draft from structured fields, with required PM review
Tradeoff
We gave up some time savings to keep the review step. In return, every executive update had an accountable author, and the model only summarized fields it was given. It never inferred status on its own.

Retrospective

What I'd do differently

Write down the evaluation for the AI drafts. Reviewers caught errors, but we didn't log what kinds of errors they were. A simple log of corrections per draft would have shown where the prompt needed work, and would have made the accuracy case to executives with data.

Bring in tool owners before discovery, not during it. At one client, an internal tool owner joined late and reopened field mappings that had already been agreed.
