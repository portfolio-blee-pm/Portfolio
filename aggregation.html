Context & my role

Three pilot markets and a national mandate

Recyclops ran a gig-aggregation model for recycling pickup: local drivers and partners, coordinated through a central platform. Three markets were live, and leadership wanted national coverage.

At the same time, the platform underneath was being rebuilt. Expansion and the rebuild had to share the same engineers, the same backlog, and the same release schedule.

I owned
The expansion program: market sequencing, the launch framework, readiness gates, and the go/no-go decision for each market launch.
I influenced
Engineering backlog priority and release sequencing for the platform rebuild, plus capacity and resource allocation across markets.
Partners
Product, Engineering, Operations, Finance, local driver and partner networks, and systems vendors.
Problem & constraints

Every launch was a one-off

Before the framework, each market launch was planned from scratch. Dependencies lived in people's heads, and the rebuild competed with expansion for the same engineering time.

No standard launch sequence or readiness criteria
Dependencies and critical path tracked ad hoc
Platform rebuild and expansion competing for one engineering team
Launch, dispatch, and billing data spread across disconnected tools
Key decisions

Two calls that shaped the program

One delivery plan for the rebuild and the expansion

Options
(a) Freeze expansion until the rebuild ships · (b) Run them as two separate programs · (c) Merge both into one plan with shared release sequencing
Chose
One plan: Scrum and Kanban teams working inside a waterfall release schedule
Tradeoff
This added planning overhead and forced some hard backlog trade-offs every week. In return, expansion never waited on the rebuild, and no release surprised the markets that depended on it.

Standardize before speeding up

Options
(a) Launch as many markets as fast as possible and fix problems afterwards · (b) Build the launch framework and gates first, then compress the cycle
Chose
Framework and gates first
Tradeoff
The first expansion markets moved more slowly. After that, each launch reused the same sequence, so time-to-launch halved and several markets could run in parallel safely.
Execution

How the rollout ran

The framework turned the national strategy into a sequence any market team could follow.

Market sequencing
Ranked markets by readiness and value, then built a rolling sequence so the next markets were already being prepared while the current ones launched.
Dependency maps & critical path
Every launch had a dependency map and a named critical path. A slip in vendor, partner, or engineering work showed up early enough to act on.
Readiness gates with owners
Four gates, each with exit criteria and one accountable owner. I made the final go/no-go call for each market.
Scenario modeling for capacity
Built scenario and resource-allocation models across all 25 markets. These drove staffing and capacity decisions and cut operating costs 18%.
Artifact

Launch readiness gates

The gate model every market went through. Each gate had one accountable owner, and no market launched with an unowned risk.

Retrospective

What I'd do differently

Put the gate criteria into the systems sooner. Early on, readiness was checked by hand in reviews. Once launch status synced automatically across tools (see Launch Systems Integration), reviews got faster. I'd build that integration in the first month, not partway through.

Document the handoff to steady state from day one. Late in the program, handoffs to steady-state ops depended too much on the people who had launched each market. A standard hypercare-exit checklist would have made that cleaner.
