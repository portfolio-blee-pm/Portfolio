Context & ownership

Two channels, two systems, one revenue number

YETI sold through B2B wholesale and direct-to-consumer. Quotes lived in Salesforce and orders and invoices in SAP, and the two often disagreed about revenue. Forecasts inherited every one of those conflicts.

I owned
Requirements, delivery plan, testing, and rollout for the CPQ rebuild. I also defined the reporting pipeline and the reconciliation logic.
I influenced
Pricing and approval policy (owned by Sales and Finance) and how enablement operated across six business units.
Partnered with
Sales, Finance, Operations, Salesforce and SAP administrators, and BI.
Problem & constraints

Slow quotes and forecasts nobody fully trusted

Pricing and approval logic too complex for the existing CPQ setup
Revenue records conflicting between Salesforce and SAP
Forecast accuracy at 78%
Revenue leakage from mismatched pricing and orders
Key decisions

Decisions I owned

Translate policy into rules, not into new exceptions

Options
(a) Rebuild the existing approval paths one-for-one · (b) Rewrite pricing and approval policy as explicit CPQ rules with fewer manual exceptions
Chose
Explicit rules with fewer exceptions
Tradeoff
It took harder conversations with Sales leadership about which exceptions were really needed. In return, approvals got faster and the rules could be tested.

Fix the data before redesigning the dashboards

Options
(a) Rebuild the Tableau dashboards on the existing feeds · (b) Define reconciliation logic between Salesforce and SAP first
Chose
Reconciliation first
Tradeoff
Dashboards waited a few cycles. When they shipped, Finance and Sales were finally looking at the same number.

What I'd do differently next time

Automate the reconciliation checks. The logic worked, but some checks were still run by hand at period close. Scheduled exception reports would have kept accuracy from slipping between closes.
