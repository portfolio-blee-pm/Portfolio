Context & ownership

A long program, measured in adoption

FIFCO USA sold through both retail and distributor channels. The legacy systems behind them made it hard to grow e-commerce and to see the business as a whole.

Replacing them was a multiyear program. Its success depended as much on whether Sales, Operations, and Marketing actually changed how they worked as on the technology itself.

I owned
The business case and executive approval, requirements, release sequencing, go-live readiness, and later the P&L and roadmap as Product Owner.
I influenced
Investment levels, vendor-contract decisions, and how each function redesigned its processes.
Partnered with
Executive leadership, Sales, Operations, Engineering, Marketing, and system vendors.

Key decisions

Decisions I owned

Migrate in phases by channel instead of all at once

Options
(a) One big-bang cutover across all channels · (b) Phased go-lives, one channel at a time
Chose
Phased go-lives by channel
Tradeoff
The program took longer end to end. In return, each go-live was smaller and safer, and lessons from one channel carried into the next.

Cut underperforming initiatives to protect margin

Options
(a) Keep funding every initiative on the roadmap · (b) Use OKRs to stop work that wasn't paying off
Chose
Stop what isn't working, and fund what is
Tradeoff
Some stakeholders lost projects they cared about. In return, the budget went to the work that drove revenue, and revenue targets were exceeded.
Execution

Operating mechanisms I put in place

Four mechanisms kept a long program on track and made the change stick.

Business case & approval
Built the investment case and secured executive approval, then reported results to leadership at every phase.
Phased release sequencing
Sequenced go-lives across retail and distributor channels, directing vendors, Engineering, and the business functions through each one.
Organizational readiness
Process redesign, role-based training, and documentation for Sales, Operations, and Marketing before each go-live.
Adoption tracking & OKRs
Tracked adoption after each go-live, and used OKRs as Product Owner to decide what to keep funding.

What I'd do differently next time

Track adoption from the first go-live, not after it. The functions that adopted fastest were the ones whose metrics we watched from day one. I'd set adoption targets before each go-live, not after.
