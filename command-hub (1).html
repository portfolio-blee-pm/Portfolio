Situation

25 markets. No shared view of any of them.

As the gig-aggregation platform scaled nationally, a second problem emerged: the organization had no centralized way to see what was happening across the portfolio. Each market reported differently. KPIs lived in separate tools. Executive visibility required manual aggregation — a process that was slow, inconsistent, and error-prone.

Leadership was making decisions on data that was days old, siloed by team, and impossible to reconcile across markets.

"We were running 25 markets and reading them like 25 separate businesses. The coordination overhead alone was becoming a drag on decision velocity — and decisions at scale are the whole game."

Fragmented reporting across teams and tools
No real-time KPI visibility at the executive layer
Manual aggregation creating latency in decision-making
No standardized operational definitions across markets
Approach

One platform. Three layers of intelligence.

The Command Hub wasn't a dashboard project — it was an operational architecture decision. The design question wasn't "what do we show?" but "what does each layer of the organization need to act on, and how fast do they need it?"

Data Layer
Unified program data model — standardized definitions, ingestion pipelines, and a single source of truth replacing fragmented market-level reporting across 25+ distributed teams.
Operations Layer
Real-time KPI dashboards — live market health indicators, SLA tracking, incident status, and dependency flags surfaced to operational leads without manual aggregation.
Executive Layer
Decision intelligence surface — portfolio-level visibility, cross-market trend analysis, and synthesized status briefings that reduced coordination overhead and accelerated strategic decisions.
Design Decisions

What made it work

⬡
Standardization First
Before any dashboard was built, every operational metric was defined, debated, and agreed on across teams. Without semantic alignment, unified visibility is just unified noise.
◈
Audience-Specific Views
Ops leads, market managers, and executives needed fundamentally different things. The platform served three distinct views from a single data model — no one got a dashboard that wasn't designed for them.
◎
Latency as a Design Constraint
Real-time wasn't a feature request — it was a business requirement. Decisions delayed by stale data cost money. Every pipeline was designed around minimum viable latency for each decision type.
