How I lead

Accountable for outcomes.
Not just plans.

Six disciplines I bring to every program, from framing the problem to sustaining the result. Each one is backed by a measured outcome.

Systems thinking

I see a program as an operating system, not a project plan: people, platforms, data, and decisions, and how each constrains the others. I find the binding constraint and design the mechanism that removes it. At Recyclops that meant running a platform rebuild and a national expansion as one integrated program, so neither stalled the other.

Dependency maps · constraints · operating mechanisms

Strategy

I tie every program decision to business value and make the trade-offs others defer. I originated the Recyclops expansion model for new enterprise offerings, paused KT Impact go-lives to standardize integrations, a short-term cost that made every later launch 35% faster, and established C-suite decision rights that tied investment to KPIs.

Expansion strategy · portfolio governance · investment priorities

Technology

I bring the technical judgment to lead engineering decisions, not just track them. I set the integration architecture, from API contracts and data schemas to the rules that keep systems in agreement, and build parts of it hands-on. That architecture connected five systems at Recyclops and lifted forecast accuracy from 78% to 91% at YETI.

APIs · schemas · data quality · SQL · Python · Salesforce · SAP

Ownership

I take single-point accountability for outcomes, including the ones I don't directly control. I've held go/no-go authority across 25+ market launches, led a team of project managers, controlled budget, vendors, and roadmap at KT Impact, and carried P&L authority as Product Owner at FIFCO.

Single accountable owner · people leader · P&L authority

Execution

I install the operating cadence that turns strategy into predictable results: readiness gates with named owners, one cross-team triage ranked by customer impact, and disciplined go/no-go decisions. The result was 25+ markets in nine months, 60% ahead of roadmap, with zero P0/P1 incidents.

Readiness gates · triage · go/no-go

Delivery

I measure success by sustained adoption and business impact, not launch dates. Release governance and SLA thresholds took a new platform to 10+ enterprise accounts with downtime cut from 8.5% to 6.6%. Phased cutover and change management held adoption at 90% after handoff.

Release cadence · cutover · hypercare · adoption

Operating model

Systems thinking,
turned into process.

I treat every program as a system: map it, find the binding constraint, design the mechanism that removes it, and measure the result. Then I institutionalize it, so the capability stays with the organization after I hand off.

01

Contracts before code.

I paused new go-lives to define API contracts and schemas for three external data feeds. Every account after reused the same path.

−35% time-to-launch
02

Gates, not heroics.

Every market passed four readiness gates, each with one accountable owner. Launches fail at the handoffs, so I engineered the handoffs.

0 P0/P1 across 25+ markets
03

Rank by customer impact.

One weekly cross-team triage, ranked by what customers felt, replaced each team triaging its own issues.

8.5% → 6.6% downtime
04

Automate assembly, keep judgment.

An LLM drafts status reports from structured fields; a PM reviews and publishes. Every update keeps an accountable author.

−50% reporting effort
