Operating model

Systems thinking,
turned into process.

I map the program as a system, find the constraint, design a mechanism that removes it, and measure whether it worked. Then I standardize it so the organization keeps it after I hand off.

01

Contracts before code.

I paused new go-lives to define API contracts and schemas for three external data feeds. Every account after reused the same path.

−35% time-to-launch
02

Gates, not heroics.

Every market passed four readiness gates, each with one accountable owner. Launches fail at the handoffs, so I designed the handoffs.

0 P0/P1 across 25+ markets
03

Rank by customer impact.

One weekly cross-team triage, ranked by what customers felt, replaced each team triaging its own issues.

8.5% → 6.6% downtime
04

Automate assembly, keep judgment.

An LLM drafts status reports from structured fields; a PM reviews and publishes. Every update keeps an accountable author.

−50% reporting effort

About

Program manager.
Systems thinker.

Every complex program comes down to agreement: between systems that must exchange data, and between people who must commit to the same version of the truth. I've spent fourteen years building that agreement, and then the operating structure that lets an organization act on it at scale.

My work sits where platform engineering, enterprise systems, and operations meet. I carried a commercial platform from development into enterprise production. I took ReDyper from concept to 25+ markets in nine months without a single P0/P1 incident, owning everything from the API layer to frontline operations. I rebuilt revenue systems across Salesforce and SAP that lifted forecast accuracy from 78% to 91%. And I governed delivery for Series A–C companies running 15–20 initiatives at once.

One conviction runs through all of it: technology should assemble the facts, and people should own the judgment. I'm pursuing Senior Program Manager roles on platform, infrastructure, and enterprise-systems teams, where clarity under complexity is the job.
