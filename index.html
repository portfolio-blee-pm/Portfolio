Systems Thinking

Systems thinking, turned into process.

Every program is a system of people, dependencies, data, and decisions. I map it, find the constraint, design a mechanism that removes it, and measure whether it worked. Then I standardize that mechanism into a process the organization keeps after I hand it off. Four examples, each with a result behind it:

01 — Contracts before code
Agree on the interface first.
At KT Impact, I paused new enterprise go-lives to define API contracts and schemas for three external data feeds. Every account after that reused the same path, and time-to-launch dropped 35%.
Enterprise platform →
02 — Gates, not heroics
Launches fail at the handoffs.
At Recyclops, every ReDyper market went through four readiness gates, and each gate had one accountable owner. Across 25+ markets, launches caused zero P0/P1 incidents.
Recyclops program →
03 — Rank by customer impact
8.5→6.6%Downtime
One triage, one priority list.
Weekly cross-team triage, ranked by what customers felt, replaced each team triaging its own issues. Production downtime fell from 8.5% to 6.6%.
Enterprise platform →
04 — Automate assembly, keep judgment
50%Less reporting effort
AI drafts, people decide.
Status reports drafted by an LLM from structured fields, then reviewed and published by a PM. Reporting overhead dropped 50% and every update still had an accountable author.

About

Program manager.
Systems thinker.

Every complex program is, at its core, a question of agreement: between systems that must exchange data, and between people who must commit to the same version of the truth. Brandon Lee has spent fourteen years building that agreement, and then the operating architecture that lets an organization act on it at scale.

He approaches every program as a system, and his work sits where platform engineering, enterprise systems, and operations meet. He carried a commercial platform from development into enterprise production. He took ReDyper, a new service model on a new gig-aggregation platform, from concept to more than twenty-five markets in nine months without a single P0/P1 incident, owning everything from the API layer to frontline operations. He rebuilt revenue systems across Salesforce and SAP that lifted forecast accuracy from 78% to 91%. He governed delivery for Series A–C companies running fifteen to twenty initiatives at once.

One conviction runs through all of it: technology should assemble the facts, and people should own the judgment. That is why his launches ran on readiness gates rather than heroics, and why his AI-assisted reporting cut the effort in half while keeping a person accountable for every update that went out.

An MBA in IT Management from WGU (2026) formalized what the work had already taught. Brandon is pursuing Senior Program Manager roles on platform, infrastructure, and enterprise-systems teams, where clarity under complexity is the job itself.
